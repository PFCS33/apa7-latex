Open with the problem your paper addresses and why it matters. Cite a
single work in parentheses \parencite{smith2021} or name the authors in
the sentence, as \textcite{nguyen2019} did. Group several sources in one
citation \parencite{smith2021,nguyen2019,who2023}, and add a page number
for direct quotations: \enquote{placeholder quotation text}
\parencite[p.~42]{nguyen2019}.

Quotations of 40 words or more go in a block quotation. Name the author
in the sentence, as \textcite{nguyen2019} did, and put only the page
number after the quotation:

\begin{quote}
Use a block quotation for any direct quotation of 40 words or more. Indent
the whole passage 0.5 inch from the left margin, keep it double-spaced, and
leave out the quotation marks. Place the citation after the final
punctuation mark, unlike a short quotation, where the citation comes before
the period. (p.~57)
\end{quote}

End the introduction with your research question or hypotheses.
