# APA 7 Student Paper Format Requirements

Summary of the rules shown in the assignment's reference document,
*APA 7 Student Sample Paper (2026)* (Purdue OWL). Use this as a checklist
when writing or reviewing a paper built from this template.

## Page Setup

- 1-inch margins on all sides.
- Double-spaced throughout, including references.
- Font: Times New Roman 12 pt, as used in the sample.
- Page number in the top-right corner of every page, starting on the title page.
- No running head for student papers. Your instructor may ask for one, or
  for your last name before the page number.

## Title Page

- Title: bold, centered, title case, 3–4 blank lines below the top margin.
- One double-spaced blank line between the title and the author names.
- Then, each centered on its own line:
  1. Author name(s): First name, middle initial(s), last name.
     Two authors are joined with "and".
  2. Affiliation: usually the department offering the course and the university.
  3. Course number and name.
  4. Instructor name and title.
  5. Due date.
- Student papers do **not** need an author note, abstract, or keywords
  unless the instructor asks for them.

## Body Text

- Repeat the paper title, bold and centered, at the top of the first text page.
- Do **not** add an "Introduction" heading.
- Indent the first line of each paragraph by 0.5 inch.

### Headings

| Level | Format                                     |
|-------|--------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Centered, bold, title case                 |
| 2     | Flush left, bold, title case               |
| 3     | Flush left, bold italic, title case        |

- Text after any heading starts as a new, indented paragraph.
- Most student papers need only Levels 1–3 (Levels 4–5 exist if needed).
- Common Level 1 sections: Literature Review, Method(s), Results, Discussion.

### Quotations

- Short quotations: in quotation marks, with page number in the citation,
  for example (Theall, 2017, p. 91).
- Quotations of **more than 40 words**: block quotation.
  - Indent the whole block 0.5 inch.
  - No quotation marks.
  - Page number(s) go after the final punctuation, for example (pp. 8–9).
  - If the author or year is not named in the text, put them in the
    parenthetical with the page number.

### Lists

- Bulleted lists are acceptable in the body.

## In-Text Citations

- Two authors: use "&" inside parentheses (Remmers & Brandenburg, 1927),
  "and" in running text.
- Three or more authors: first author + "et al." (Boring et al., 2017).
  The period goes after "al". This applies only to in-text citations.
- Several sources in one parenthesis: alphabetical, separated by semicolons
  (Gaff & Simpson, 1994; Lewis, 1996; McKeachie, 1996).
- Same author, several years: list the years separated by commas
  (Knoch, 2007, 2009).
- Secondary source: Seldin (1993, as cited in Pounder, 2007). Only the
  secondary source (Pounder) goes in the reference list.
- Organization as author: cite the organization name (US Department of Education).
- Examples of a larger body of work: (see, e.g., Bloom et al., 1956; Cashin, 1990).
- Several quotations from one source: put each page number right after its
  quotation, for example "grade leniently" (p. 1365).
- Paraphrases need no page number; a page or range may be added if it helps.

## Writing Style

- Spell out an abbreviation the first time you use it, followed by the
  abbreviation in parentheses: student evaluation of teacher (SET).
  Very well-known abbreviations are exempt.
- Numbers:
  - Words for zero to nine, numerals for 10 and above.
  - Always numerals for percentages, ratios, dates, and scale values.
  - Always words when a number begins a sentence, title, or heading.
  - See APA 7 manual sections 6.32–6.35 for exceptions.
- Decimals: put a leading zero before the decimal point only when the value
  can exceed 1 (0.34 standard deviations). No leading zero for values that
  cannot exceed 1, such as proportions or correlations.
- Italicize scale anchors and responses (*strongly agree*, *undecided*),
  unless the responses are numbered.
- Put verbatim test items or instructions to participants in quotation marks.

## Tables and Figures

- Number tables and figures separately and in order of first mention
  (Table 1 can come after Figure 1).
- Refer to every table and figure in the text.
- Above the table or figure:
  1. Number: bold, flush left (**Table 1**, **Figure 1**).
  2. Title: italic, title case, on the next line.
- Below the table or figure: a note starting with the italic label *Note.*
  - General notes first, then specific notes marked with superscript
    lowercase letters.
  - Notes are optional.
- Tables: clean and easy to read, with as few borders as possible
  (typically lines above and below the header row and at the bottom).
  No shading unless it carries meaning, which must be explained in the note.
- A table that runs over several pages repeats its column headers on each page.

## References

- Start on a new page.
- Heading "References" (or "Reference" for only one source): bold, centered.
- Entries in alphabetical order, double-spaced.
- Hanging indent of 0.5 inch: the first line is flush left, following lines
  are indented. Use the word processor's or LaTeX's hanging indent, not tabs.
- Every source cited in the text has a reference entry, and vice versa.
- Up to 20 authors: list them all. 21 or more: first 19, an ellipsis, then
  the final author.
- Include a DOI or stable URL for online sources when available.
- Organization as author: the entry starts with the organization name.
- Undated sources use (n.d.).

## Appendices

- Come after the reference list. Start each appendix on a new page.
- Label: "Appendix", bold and centered, at the top of the page.
- One appendix: label it "Appendix". Several: "Appendix A", "Appendix B", and so on.
- Refer to appendices in the text ("see Appendix", "see Appendix A").
- An appendix containing **only** one table or figure: the bold centered
  appendix label replaces the usual "Table X" label, and the italic title
  follows directly.
- An appendix with text **and** tables or figures: label them with the
  appendix letter (Table A1, Table A2, Figure A1; Table B1 for Appendix B).
  With only one appendix, still use "A" (Table A1).
- Paragraphs in an appendix are indented as in the body.
